# Air Quality Analysis and Decision-Support System

Analysis of daily AQI for **Delhi, Mumbai and Bengaluru** (May 2015 - Dec 2023), built on AQI observations derived
from CPCB daily bulletins. The notebook covers data quality, trends, seasonality, extreme days, city comparison, a
weather-AQI relationship (Delhi and Bengaluru), a heuristic risk score, a linear trend projection, an illustrative
scenario analysis and a rule-based alert demonstration.

Every section is tagged **EMPIRICAL** (computed from observations), **PROJECTION / HEURISTIC** or **SCENARIO**
(assumption-driven), and these are not mixed.

**Provenance checks**

- The AQI file is the UrbanEmissions repository file `data/Processed/AllIndiaBulletins_Master.csv`
  (github.com/urbanemissionsinfo/AQI_bulletins). Its MD5 (`7a3173948315bdf8ac97701f9fab5d06`) equals the MD5 listed on
  the Zenodo record for `India_AQI_Bulletins_Master.csv`; the notebook re-checks this on every run.
- The weather files contain no city column. Their city comes from provenance: the original project notebook downloaded
  two OpenCity URLs whose resource IDs (`9cf80863-...`, `0c853240-...`) are listed by OpenCity as the Bengaluru and Delhi
  daily temperature files, saved in that order as `weather_1`/`weather_2`. Monthly temperature climatology agrees
  (Delhi cold in January and hot in May; Bengaluru milder, peaking in April). The files were re-saved by pandas, so
  a hash comparison with OpenCity was not possible. **There is no Mumbai weather file**, so Mumbai is excluded from weather analysis.
- OpenCity lists the dataset (one licence for all its resources) and the Delhi resource page under the licence
  "Other (Public Domain)" (checked 14 October 2026). This is OpenCity's own label for data it lists as sourced from IMD;
  IMD's own terms, and those of Ogimet (named by OpenCity as the source of the station data from 2021), were not checked.
- **`Rain` column.** OpenCity's dataset description covers temperature only ("max, min in Degree Celsius"), and its data
  dictionary lists `Rain` as a text column with no description, unit or source. The rainfall unit, and whether `Rain` comes from
  the same IMD series as the temperatures, are therefore unverified. The notebook uses `Rain` only through rank correlations and a
  rain >= 1 (file units) threshold, and the rain results below should be read with this caveat.

## Cleaning and data quality (all reported in notebook Section 3)

- Kept cities exactly as named in the source (Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru; "Navi Mumbai" is a separate city and not merged).
  The source already uses "Bengaluru", so no renaming was needed.
- No rows were added, deleted or imputed. 0 duplicate rows, 0 missing AQI values. 25 rows have no station count.
- Minor standardisation: "Very poor" -> "Very Poor" (5 rows); station counts parsed to integers; AQI category derived
  from the AQI value using CPCB bounds (12 of 9,147 reported labels differ from their value; originals are kept).
- Weather: "Tr" (trace rain) treated as 0; placeholder entries (`-----`) treated as missing; 3 Delhi rows with
  unparseable dates (all after the AQI period) dropped.

## Empirical results (observed bulletin AQI, 2015-2023)

| | Delhi | Mumbai | Bengaluru |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean / median AQI | 217 / 209 | 108 / 91 | 75 / 69 |
| 95th percentile AQI | 399 | 214 | 132 |
| Days with AQI > 200 | 52.4% | 7.7% | 0.5% |
| Days with AQI > 300 | 24.2% (761 days) | 0.1% (2 days) | 0% |
| Longest run of AQI > 300 | 32 days (from 2017-10-17) | 1 day | - |
| Winter / monsoon mean AQI | 305 / 127 | 167 / 60 | 93 / 54 |

- All three cities show a clear seasonal cycle: highest in winter (Delhi also in post-monsoon; its highest-mean month is November), lowest in the monsoon.
- Delhi's yearly mean fell from 256 (2016) to 204 (2023), with the lowest value in 2020 (185); Mumbai's yearly mean rose from about 94 to about 120.
  The number of reporting stations changed a lot over the period (Delhi 5 -> 36, Mumbai 1 -> 19), so some of this may reflect the monitoring network.
- **Weather (Delhi, Bengaluru; associations only, no causal claim).** Raw correlations mostly reflect the shared annual cycle;
  after removing each city's monthly mean, Spearman correlations with AQI are small: Delhi max temp +0.34, min temp +0.10, rain -0.14;
  Bengaluru max temp +0.28, min temp -0.10, rain -0.17. On rainy vs dry non-monsoon days, mean AQI was 161 vs 270 (Delhi) and 67 vs 90 (Bengaluru).

## Projection and heuristic (not validated)

- **Linear Trend Projection (2024-2026):** a straight line fitted to each city's yearly mean AQI (7-8 well-covered years), with a +/- 1
  residual-standard-error band. Slopes: Delhi -6.4, Mumbai +3.2, Bengaluru -1.6 AQI/year; 2026 values 175, 128 and 66.
  This is an extrapolation of a few points, not a tested forecasting model; the bulletin file ends in 2023, so it cannot be checked here.
- **Heuristic Risk Score** (0-100): mean of per-day category points (Good 0 ... Severe 5) scaled to 100. The equal weights are a project-defined
  choice, not an official or validated index. Scores: Delhi 52.9, Mumbai 28.1, Bengaluru 19.6.

## Scenario analysis (assumptions, not findings)

- **Illustrative Policy Scenario:** observed daily AQI scaled by an assumed uniform 10 / 20 / 30% reduction to show how threshold exceedance
  shares would change *if* such a reduction occurred. Reductions are arbitrary levels, not estimates of any real policy; no policy is ranked.
- **Rule-Based AQI Alert Demonstration:** four project-defined AQI-threshold levels replayed over the observed history. Not GRAP stages and not a real-time system.
- The earlier ROI table and per-person health-cost model were removed: all of their inputs were unsourced assumptions and their outputs only restated those inputs.

## Limitations

- City-average AQI comes from a changing, often small number of stations (Mumbai had 1 station until 2018); trends and cross-city comparisons are affected.
- The bulletins start in May 2015 (Mumbai in August 2015) and end in December 2023; Mumbai has the most gaps.
- Weather covers Delhi and Bengaluru only; OpenCity notes gridded IMD data up to 2020 and station data from 2021, so a level shift is possible. No wind or humidity data.
- Weather results are associations on daily data and do not establish cause.
- The trend projection and risk score are simple and unvalidated; scenario outputs depend entirely on their stated assumptions.
